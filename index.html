Why German matters

German is more than a visa requirement—it is your everyday advantage.

Many people can begin in English, especially in international teams. German becomes increasingly important when you deal with employers, authorities, neighbours, healthcare, education and long-term life in Germany.

💼

More career opportunities

German expands the number of roles you can apply for and helps you communicate naturally with colleagues, customers and suppliers.

🏛️

Handle official matters

Understand letters, appointments, forms and conversations with local authorities, insurers and public services.

🎓

Study and training access

German-taught degree programmes and vocational training commonly require documented language skills.

🩺

Regulated professions

Healthcare, teaching, law and other regulated professions may require specific general or professional German levels.

🤝

Build real connections

Join clubs, speak with neighbours, participate in school life and feel more confident in your local community.

🏡

Settle for the long term

German supports integration and is relevant for several permanent-residence and citizenship pathways, depending on your case.

CEFR roadmap

What do A1, A2, B1, B2, C1 and C2 actually mean?

The Common European Framework of Reference for Languages uses six levels and practical "can do" descriptions. Select a level to see what it means for real life.

A1 · Beginner

Start with essential everyday German.

A1
Understand

Very familiar words, simple notices and slow, clear questions about personal information.

Communicate

Introduce yourself, order something, give basic details and ask simple questions with support.

Typical use

First arrival, basic shopping, simple appointments and an initial foundation for certain visa routes.

Next target

Build routine vocabulary, basic grammar and confidence in short conversations.

A practical A1 goal

Hold a two-minute introduction, understand simple directions and complete a basic personal-information form.

Visa, study and profession

How much German might you need?

Legal residence requirements, university admission, professional recognition and employer expectations are different questions. Always check the rule for your exact route.

PathwayGeneral legal language positionPractical guidance
Qualified-professional work visaNo general legal minimumEmployers or recognition authorities may still require German, especially for customer-facing or regulated work.
EU Blue CardNo general legal minimumGerman improves workplace integration and can affect later settlement-permit timelines.
Opportunity Card – points routeA1 German or B2 EnglishEligibility can differ when the qualification is already fully recognised in Germany.
Recognition of foreign qualificationsUsually at least A2The profession-specific recognition procedure may demand a higher level.
Recognition partnershipAt least A2Continue recognition and employment steps after arrival under the applicable conditions.
Vocational trainingAt least B1Training takes place mainly in German at the company and vocational school.
Seeking a vocational-training placeAt least B1You must be able to apply, interview and prepare for training in German.
German-language university studyOften B2 or higherThe university and programme decide which certificate and score are accepted.
Research residence routeNo general legal minimumThe institution, project language and daily duties determine the practical level needed.
Healthcare and regulated professionsOften B1/B2 or specialist levelRequirements depend on the profession, recognition body and federal state.
!

Important distinction

"No legal minimum for the visa" does not mean "no German needed for the job." Employers, universities and professional-recognition authorities may apply separate requirements.

Choose your learning route

Learn before arrival, after arrival—or both.

The strongest plan begins in your home country, then combines structured learning with daily practice once you are in Germany.

🌍
01

Start in your home country

Build a foundation before your visa, interview, university or training process begins.

  • Goethe-Institut and recognised local partners
  • Online teacher-led courses
  • Self-study with structured free platforms
  • Exam preparation with official sample tests
💻
02

Learn online

Combine video, audio, reading, writing feedback and live speaking practice around your schedule.

  • Deutsche Welle learning programmes
  • VHS-Lernportal A1–B2
  • Goethe exercises and communities
  • Podcasts, news and digital libraries
🇩🇪
03

Continue in Germany

Choose local courses that fit your residence status, work schedule and learning goal.

  • Integration courses
  • Adult education centres (Volkshochschule)
  • Goethe-Institut courses and exams
  • Private language schools and university courses
🧰
04

Learn German for work

Move beyond textbook German and practise the situations you face in your profession.

  • BAMF vocational language courses
  • Job-related vocabulary and communication
  • Industry-specific modules
  • Employer-supported or in-company learning

Integration courses

Learn the language—and understand life in Germany.

Integration courses combine German for everyday life with an orientation course on Germany's legal system, history, culture and social values.

A structured route to everyday independence.

The standard general integration course normally consists of a language course and an orientation course. Special formats are available for certain groups, including women, parents, young people and learners with literacy needs.

600standard language-course teaching units
100orientation-course teaching units

Successful completion generally involves the language test and the "Living in Germany" orientation test. Participation rights, obligations, fees and exemptions depend on personal circumstances.

1

Check your eligibility

Ask the foreigners authority, Jobcenter or BAMF whether you are entitled or required to participate.

2

Find a provider

Use BAMF-NAvI or contact a migration advisory service to find approved courses nearby.

3

Take the placement test

The provider identifies the correct course level and suitable course format.

4

Complete language and orientation modules

Learn practical German for work, authorities, shopping, family life and communication.

5

Take the final tests

Successful participants receive the integration-course certificate.

Recognised certificates

Choose the exam that matches your purpose.

For visa procedures, use certificates accepted by the competent authority. For university admission, verify the exact certificate and score directly with the institution.

A simple starting plan

Your first 30 days of German.

Consistency beats occasional intensive study. Use a small daily system that trains all four skills.

Week 1

Build the foundation

  • Learn pronunciation and greetings
  • Introduce yourself
  • Master numbers, dates and time
  • Study 15 useful words daily
Week 2

Use everyday German

  • Shopping and transport phrases
  • Ask and answer simple questions
  • Write short messages
  • Repeat one audio lesson daily
Week 3

Connect German to your goal

  • Learn study or job vocabulary
  • Practise a short self-introduction
  • Read one simple text daily
  • Join a speaking group or tandem
Week 4

Test and improve

  • Take a sample level test
  • Record your speaking
  • Review weak grammar topics
  • Choose your next course or exam
