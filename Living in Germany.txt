  <section class="e2gm-section" id="e2gm-health">
    <div class="e2gm-wrap">
      <div class="e2gm-section-head"><div><p class="e2gm-kicker">Health insurance</p><h2>Health insurance is compulsory in Germany.</h2></div><p>Germany has statutory and private systems. Eligibility, contributions and benefits differ, and switching between systems is not always simple.</p></div>
      <div class="e2gm-split">
        <article class="e2gm-panel"><h3>Statutory health insurance (GKV)</h3><p>Most employees are covered by statutory health insurance. Contributions are generally linked to income.</p><ul class="e2gm-checks"><li>Doctor and hospital treatment under the statutory benefit catalogue</li><li>Prescription medication and medical aids, subject to rules and co-payments</li><li>Pregnancy, childbirth and medically necessary rehabilitation</li><li>Eligible non-working spouses and children may be included through family insurance</li><li>Long-term care insurance is normally linked to health insurance</li></ul></article>
        <article class="e2gm-panel dark"><h3>Private health insurance (PKV)</h3><p>Private insurance may be available to eligible high earners, self-employed people and civil servants.</p><ul class="e2gm-checks"><li>Premiums are based on the tariff and personal risk factors rather than income alone</li><li>Patients commonly receive bills and claim reimbursement</li><li>Each family member usually needs a separate policy</li><li>Coverage varies significantly by tariff</li><li>Returning to statutory insurance can be difficult, especially later in life</li></ul></article>
      </div>
      <div class="e2gm-cards" style="margin-top:18px">
        <article class="e2gm-card feature"><div class="e2gm-card-icon">🪪</div><h3>Electronic health card (eGK)</h3><p>Members of statutory insurance receive an electronic health card and should take it to medical appointments.</p></article>
        <article class="e2gm-card"><div class="e2gm-card-icon">🇪🇺</div><h3>EHIC</h3><p>The European Health Insurance Card is normally printed on the back of the German statutory health card for temporary stays in participating countries.</p></article>
        <article class="e2gm-card"><div class="e2gm-card-icon">🧳</div><h3>Cover before arrival</h3><p>Temporary international or incoming insurance may be needed until your regular German cover begins and can also be relevant for the visa process.</p></article>
        <article class="e2gm-card"><div class="e2gm-card-icon">🎓</div><h3>Students and trainees</h3><p>Students, apprentices and interns are generally required to hold suitable health insurance; special rules and exceptions apply.</p></article>

  <section class="e2gm-section" id="e2gm-faq">
    <div class="e2gm-wrap">
      <div class="e2gm-section-head"><div><p class="e2gm-kicker">Frequently asked questions</p><h2>Clear answers to common first steps.</h2></div><p>These answers are general orientation. Exact rules depend on your residence status, employer, university, profession and personal circumstances.</p></div>
      <div class="e2gm-faq">
        <details><summary>Can I work in Germany without German?</summary><p>Some international roles operate mainly in English, and several work residence routes have no general statutory German minimum. Employers may nevertheless require German, and the language is highly valuable for everyday life and long-term career development.</p></details>
        <details><summary>Which German certificate is accepted?</summary><p>The answer depends on the purpose. Visa procedures generally require certificates from recognised providers. Universities and professional-recognition authorities may specify a particular exam, level or score.</p></details>
        <details><summary>Is a German bank account legally compulsory?</summary><p>A German account is not universally required by law, but it is extremely practical and commonly needed for salary payments, rent, direct debits and other everyday transactions.</p></details>
        <details><summary>Can I choose any health insurer?</summary><p>Within the statutory system, eligible members can generally choose among statutory providers. Private insurance is only available to certain groups and requires careful comparison because benefits, premiums and future switching options differ.</p></details>
        <details><summary>Is private liability insurance mandatory?</summary><p>It is voluntary, but it is widely considered important because a serious liability claim can be financially significant. Always check exclusions, insured limits and whether family members are included.</p></details>
        <details><summary>Does EHIC replace travel insurance?</summary><p>No. EHIC provides access to medically necessary state healthcare during temporary stays in participating countries under local rules. It does not necessarily cover repatriation, all private treatment or every travel-related risk.</p></details>
