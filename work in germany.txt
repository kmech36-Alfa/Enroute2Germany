      <div class="e2gw-section-head"><div><p class="e2gw-kicker">Common questions</p><h2>Clear answers for international graduates.</h2></div><p>These answers are intentionally concise. Individual cases should be checked with the competent authority.</p></div>
      <div class="e2gw-faq">
        <details><summary>Can I work during the 18-month job-search period?</summary><p>Yes. Graduates using the specific post-study job-search residence permit may take any type of employment while searching for qualified work.</p></details>
        <details><summary>Can I become a research assistant after my Master’s?</summary><p>Yes, when you secure a suitable university or research-institute contract. The correct residence title may be a research permit, skilled-worker permit, EU Blue Card or, for a full-time study doctorate, a study permit.</p></details>
        <details><summary>Does every graduate get PR after 24 months?</summary><p>No. The facilitated 24-month contribution route is for eligible people who completed higher education or vocational training in Germany and meet all other settlement-permit requirements.</p></details>
        <details><summary>Is B1 German required for every Blue Card PR route?</summary><p>No. A1 can support the 27-month Blue Card route; B1 reduces the period to 21 months. Other conditions still apply.</p></details>
        <details><summary>Do five years of pension payments guarantee citizenship?</summary><p>No. Naturalisation generally concerns five years of lawful habitual residence plus language, livelihood, civic-knowledge, residence-status and other requirements. It is not based solely on pension payments.</p></details>
        <details><summary>Are these timelines guaranteed?</summary><p>No. Laws can change, processing practices vary, and each authority checks the individual case. Always verify the current rule before applying.</p></details>
