  <section class="e2gl-section" id="e2gl-why">
    <div class="e2gl-wrap">
      <div class="e2gl-section-head">
        <div><p class="e2gl-kicker">Why German matters</p><h2>German is more than a visa requirement—it is your everyday advantage.</h2></div>
        <p>Many people can begin in English, especially in international teams. German becomes increasingly important when you deal with employers, authorities, neighbours, healthcare, education and long-term life in Germany.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="e2gl-reasons">
        <article class="e2gl-reason"><div class="e2gl-reason-icon">💼</div><h3>More career opportunities</h3><p>German expands the number of roles you can apply for and helps you communicate naturally with colleagues, customers and suppliers.</p></article>
        <article class="e2gl-reason"><div class="e2gl-reason-icon">🏛️</div><h3>Handle official matters</h3><p>Understand letters, appointments, forms and conversations with local authorities, insurers and public services.</p></article>
        <article class="e2gl-reason"><div class="e2gl-reason-icon">🎓</div><h3>Study and training access</h3><p>German-taught degree programmes and vocational training commonly require documented language skills.</p></article>
        <article class="e2gl-reason"><div class="e2gl-reason-icon">🩺</div><h3>Regulated professions</h3><p>Healthcare, teaching, law and other regulated professions may require specific general or professional German levels.</p></article>
        <article class="e2gl-reason"><div class="e2gl-reason-icon">🤝</div><h3>Build real connections</h3><p>Join clubs, speak with neighbours, participate in school life and feel more confident in your local community.</p></article>
        <article class="e2gl-reason"><div class="e2gl-reason-icon">🏡</div><h3>Settle for the long term</h3><p>German supports integration and is relevant for several permanent-residence and citizenship pathways, depending on your case.</p></article>
      </div>
    </div>

  <section class="e2gl-section" id="e2gl-requirements">
    <div class="e2gl-wrap">
      <div class="e2gl-section-head">
        <div><p class="e2gl-kicker">Visa, study and profession</p><h2>How much German might you need?</h2></div>
        <p>Legal residence requirements, university admission, professional recognition and employer expectations are different questions. Always check the rule for your exact route.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="e2gl-table-wrap">
        <table class="e2gl-table">
          <thead><tr><th>Pathway</th><th>General legal language position</th><th>Practical guidance</th></tr></thead>
          <tbody>
            <tr><td>Qualified-professional work visa</td><td><span class="e2gl-level-pill">No general legal minimum</span></td><td>Employers or recognition authorities may still require German, especially for customer-facing or regulated work.</td></tr>
            <tr><td>EU Blue Card</td><td><span class="e2gl-level-pill">No general legal minimum</span></td><td>German improves workplace integration and can affect later settlement-permit timelines.</td></tr>
            <tr><td>Opportunity Card – points route</td><td><span class="e2gl-level-pill">A1 German or B2 English</span></td><td>Eligibility can differ when the qualification is already fully recognised in Germany.</td></tr>
            <tr><td>Recognition of foreign qualifications</td><td><span class="e2gl-level-pill">Usually at least A2</span></td><td>The profession-specific recognition procedure may demand a higher level.</td></tr>
            <tr><td>Recognition partnership</td><td><span class="e2gl-level-pill">At least A2</span></td><td>Continue recognition and employment steps after arrival under the applicable conditions.</td></tr>
            <tr><td>Vocational training</td><td><span class="e2gl-level-pill">At least B1</span></td><td>Training takes place mainly in German at the company and vocational school.</td></tr>
            <tr><td>Seeking a vocational-training place</td><td><span class="e2gl-level-pill">At least B1</span></td><td>You must be able to apply, interview and prepare for training in German.</td></tr>
            <tr><td>German-language university study</td><td><span class="e2gl-level-pill">Often B2 or higher</span></td><td>The university and programme decide which certificate and score are accepted.</td></tr>
            <tr><td>Research residence route</td><td><span class="e2gl-level-pill">No general legal minimum</span></td><td>The institution, project language and daily duties determine the practical level needed.</td></tr>
            <tr><td>Healthcare and regulated professions</td><td><span class="e2gl-level-pill">Often B1/B2 or specialist level</span></td><td>Requirements depend on the profession, recognition body and federal state.</td></tr>
          </tbody>
        </table>
      </div>
      <div class="e2gl-note"><i>!</i><div><h3>Important distinction</h3><p>“No legal minimum for the visa” does not mean “no German needed for the job.” Employers, universities and professional-recognition authorities may apply separate requirements.</p></div></div>
